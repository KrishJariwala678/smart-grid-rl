Three-Node Grid Balancer

Nodes A (city), B (suburb) and C (industrial) each generate and consume power on their own daily cycle. Every simulated hour a controller may route 20 MW along one line to pull all three net imbalances toward zero. This runs the full simulator and its physics live, hour by hour, and shows every step of the decision.

▲ Reported negative result

The three PPO agents made the grid worse than leaving it alone: −14.93 % against +21.71 % for a one-line proportional rule. The learned mean policy drives each line to its capacity limit and holds it there, so the shunts stop tracking the imbalance. Each agent is rewarded only for its own node while its imbalance also depends on the neighbour's line, and a ±5 MW/hour delta cannot follow a signal whose hourly swings are several times larger. Balancing the critic loss and raising the learning rate were both measured and did not fix it, so this is reported rather than tuned away: on this problem the simple proportional rule is the better decentralized controller, and the centralized agent is better still.

How each hour is computed

  • Generate. Load and generation per node from node-specific diurnal sinusoids — solar-peaked A, wind and night-peaked B, industrial C on a 12 h load cycle — times a daily weather multiplier, plus AR(1) random-walk noise with momentum 0.85.
  • Weather. A 3-state daily Markov chain (normal / heatwave / storm). Heatwave scales load ×1.35, solar ×1.15, wind ×0.50; storm scales load ×0.90, solar ×0.15, wind ×1.80. The controllers never observe this state.
  • Rare events. Industrial demand spikes of 30–60 MW at node C (p = 0.02/h), total solar outage at A (p = 0.005/h), and suburb load collapse to 20 % at B (p = 0.01/h).
  • Observe. Net imbalance G − L per node, in MW, divided by 100 so every model input sits roughly in [−1, 1]. The controller sees a 6-hour window, nothing else.
  • Decide. One of 7 discrete actions: hold, or move the transfer amount along one of 6 directed node pairs.
  • Settle. The transfer is added to one node's imbalance and subtracted from the other. Reward is (Σ|pre| − Σ|post| − penalty × moved) / 10 — how many MW of grid stress the action actually removed, not the absolute stress level.

Limitations

  • The grid is synthetic: no real weather, demand or price data.
  • The transmission model has no losses and no line impedance — a transfer arrives in full.
  • The centralized action set allows exactly one transfer per hour, so the oracle's reduction is a genuine ceiling for that action space, not for the problem.
  • Each published figure is a single seeded run, not a mean over seeds.
  • The Transformer-DQN option in the live simulator is a behavioural emulation — the oracle's scoring with the measured 68.3 % oracle agreement and bias toward acting. It is not the trained Keras network, which cannot run in a browser. The oracle, proportional, hold and random controllers above it are the exact policies from the trained system, computed here in full.
Simulator components: generate_complex_data, FastEnv, PettingZooGridEnv, make_proportional_policy. Ported to the browser; physics, parameters and reward identical to the training environment. Seed changes the noise draw, not the model.
